Chapter 4 · Lesson 2

Alkyl Groups

Learn to recognize carbon branches, identify where they attach, and use their names when naming simple alkanes.

The ideaCommon groupsAttachmentWorked examplePractice

1. What is an alkyl group?

An alkyl group is a group obtained conceptually by removing one hydrogen atom from an alkane. The carbon where that hydrogen was removed is the attachment point: its free bond can connect the group to another atom or carbon chain.

From alkane to alkyl group

CH₄  →  CH₃–
methane   →   methyl

CH₃–CH₃  →  CH₃CH₂–
ethane   →   ethyl

The dash shows the bond available for attachment. These are a way to describe fragments; the illustration is not a recipe for making them.

Replace the ending -ane with -yl to name a simple group: methane → methyl, ethane → ethyl. An acyclic alkyl group formed this way has the formula CnH2n+1–.

Reading the formula: In –CH₂CH₃, the dash sits beside CH₂, so that carbon attaches to the rest of the molecule. The same group may be written CH₃CH₂–.

Quick check

How many carbon atoms are in an ethyl group? Which carbon is attached in –CH₂CH₃?

Show answer
Two carbon atoms. The CH₂ carbon is attached to the rest of the molecule because it sits beside the free bond.

3. Comparing the four butyl groups

Each structure below has four carbon atoms. Read from the dash, which marks the point of attachment. The four groups have different connections or attachment points.

StructureSystematic nameCommon name
–CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃butan-1-yln-butyl
–CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃butan-2-ylsec-butyl
–CH₂CH(CH₃)₂2-methylpropylisobutyl
–C(CH₃)₃2-methylpropan-2-yltert-butyl
Notice the distinction: sec-butyl attaches through an internal carbon of an unbranched four-carbon skeleton. Isobutyl attaches through an end CH₂ of a branched skeleton. An internal attachment does not by itself mean the carbon skeleton is branched.

The names sec-butyl and tert-butyl refer to an attachment carbon bonded within the group to two or three other carbons, respectively. Isobutyl attaches through a CH₂ bonded to only one other carbon within the group.

Try it · Follow the bond

Compare –CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ and –CH₂CH(CH₃)₂. Which one has an attachment carbon connected to two other carbons within the group?

Show reasoning
–CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃: the attached CH connects to CH₃ and CH₂CH₃. It is butan-2-yl (sec-butyl). In the second group, the attached CH₂ connects to just one carbon within the group.

4. From a branch to a name

A substituent replaces a hydrogen on a parent structure. For a simple acyclic alkane:

  1. Trace the longest continuous carbon chain; it may bend. If equally long chains are possible, select one with more substituents.
  2. Number the chain to give substituents the lowest set of locants, comparing at the first point of difference.
  3. Name each branch and record its position. Use di-, tri-, and so on for repeated identical branches, with a locant for each.
  4. Put different substituent names in alphabetical order. Ignore multiplying prefixes such as di- and tri- when alphabetizing. Use commas between numbers and hyphens between numbers and words.
These steps apply to the simple acyclic alkanes in this lesson; parent selection and numbering can involve additional rules for other functional groups.

Worked example · One methyl branch

CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃

Trace four continuous carbons: butane. The remaining CH₃ group is methyl. Number from the nearer end: it is on carbon 2, not carbon 3.

Name: 2-methylbutane.

In a skeletal drawing, an unlabeled line end or vertex represents a carbon, and enough hydrogens are implied to give each carbon its usual four bonds. First trace the complete parent chain; only then identify the carbon groups left outside it as substituents.

5. Worked example · Two different branches

Name the following structure. Parentheses in the condensed formula mark groups attached to the preceding carbon.

CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH(CH₂CH₃)–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃

  1. Parent: A longest continuous chain has six carbons: hexane. Of equally long choices, this chain has more substituents.
  2. Number: From the left the substituent locants are 2 and 3; from the right they are 4 and 5. Choose 2,3.
  3. Identify: C-2 has methyl (–CH₃); C-3 has ethyl (–CH₂CH₃).
  4. Assemble: Alphabetically, ethyl comes before methyl.

3-ethyl-2-methylhexane

Common mistake · Is it really a branch?

Would CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ be named 1-methylbutane?

Show answer
No. There is a continuous chain of five carbons, so the parent is pentane and there is no methyl branch. Check the longest chain before naming a side group.

Challenge · Compare groups

Write the two different propyl groups. Explain how their formulas can match while their attachment points differ.

Show answer
–CH₂CH₂CH₃ (propan-1-yl) attaches at an end carbon; –CH(CH₃)₂ (propan-2-yl) attaches at the middle carbon. Both are C₃H₇–; their connectivity to the rest of a molecule differs.

7. Key takeaways

  • An alkyl group can be represented as an alkane minus one hydrogen; the dash marks its attachment bond.
  • Count carbons and locate the attachment point before naming the group.
  • The same alkane can give different groups when hydrogen is removed from different positions.
  • In simple acyclic alkane naming, select a parent chain first, then number and name its substituents.
  • Different groups are listed alphabetically in the assembled name; locants show their positions.

Key terms

Parent chain: the selected carbon chain that supplies the base name. Substituent: an atom or group attached in place of a hydrogen. Locant: a number indicating a position. Attachment point: the atom in the group that bonds to the parent.
