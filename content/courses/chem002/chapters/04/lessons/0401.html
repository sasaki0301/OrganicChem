Alkanes and Alkane Isomers

Learn to read carbon skeletons, name simple alkanes, and decide whether two drawings represent different constitutional isomers or the same molecule.

Recall: Carbon typically forms four bonds and hydrogen one. In this lesson, a branch written in parentheses is attached to the atom immediately before the parentheses.

1. What are alkanes?

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons: they contain only carbon and hydrogen and have only single bonds. For an acyclic (open-chain) alkane, whether straight or branched, the molecular formula is:

CnH2n+2   (n ≥ 1)

Each single C–C or C–H bond is a σ bond. Carbon atoms in ordinary alkanes are described by the sp³ model, with approximately tetrahedral local bonding geometry. A straight carbon chain describes connectivity; it does not mean the atoms form a straight line in three dimensions.

Worked example 1 · From carbon count to formula

Find the formula for an acyclic alkane with six carbons

  1. Set n = 6.
  2. Calculate H = 2(6) + 2 = 14.
  3. The molecular formula is C₆H₁₄. Different acyclic connectivities can share this formula.
Scope: CnH2n+2 applies to acyclic alkanes, not all hydrocarbons. An unsubstituted monocycloalkane has CnH2n; an acyclic alkene can have that formula too. A molecular formula alone does not always uniquely reveal the structure.

Quick check 1

What is the molecular formula of an acyclic alkane with five carbon atoms?

Show answer and explanation
With n = 5, H = 2(5) + 2 = 12. The formula is C₅H₁₂. This formula has several possible carbon skeletons.

2. Read carbon structures

RepresentationExample for propaneWhat it tells you
MolecularC₃H₈Atom counts; it does not by itself show connectivity.
CondensedCH₃CH₂CH₃Connected groups written in a compact order.
SkeletalTwo connected line segmentsThree carbon positions at two ends and one corner; C–H bonds are usually omitted.

In a skeletal formula, an unlabeled endpoint, corner, or junction represents a carbon. Add enough implicit H atoms to give each neutral carbon four bonds. A labeled atom such as O is the atom shown, not an extra carbon.

Worked example 2 · Decode a condensed formula

CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃

  1. Start with the sequence CH₃–CH–CH₃.
  2. The parenthesized CH₃ is attached to the preceding middle CH.
  3. Count: 4 C atoms and 3 + 1 + 3 + 3 = 10 H, so the molecular formula is C₄H₁₀.

The middle carbon is connected to three other carbons. That connectivity matters when deciding whether another drawing is the same molecule.

3. Constitutional isomers

Constitutional isomers have the same molecular formula but different atom-to-atom connectivity. They are different compounds. Merely rotating a drawing or rotating around a single bond changes how the same structure is shown; it does not create a new constitutional isomer.

Worked example 3 · C₄H₁₀

Two carbon skeletons

NameCondensed structureCarbon connectivity
ButaneCH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃Four carbons in one chain.
2-Methylpropane (isobutane)CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃One carbon is attached to three others.

Both are C₄H₁₀. Because their carbon connections differ, they are constitutional isomers. These are the two constitutional isomers of C₄H₁₀.

Quick check 2 · Same or different?

Are CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ and CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ drawn in the opposite direction different isomers?

Show answer and explanation
No. Reading the same four-carbon chain from the other end leaves all atom connections unchanged. Both are butane.

4. Generate and name the C₅H₁₂ isomers

Use a systematic search: start with the longest possible chain, then make shorter eligible chains with branches. Each time, check that the selected parent is the longest continuous chain, count all carbons, and remove duplicate connectivities.

Worked example 4 · Three distinct skeletons
NameCondensed formulaHow to recognize it
PentaneCH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃Five-carbon continuous chain.
2-MethylbutaneCH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃Four-carbon parent, methyl on carbon 2.
2,2-DimethylpropaneC(CH₃)₄Three-carbon parent; two methyl groups at carbon 2.

These are the three constitutional isomers of C₅H₁₂. In the last formula, a central carbon is attached to four CH₃ groups; the longest continuous path contains three carbons.

Naming focus · Compare numbering

Why not 3-methylbutane?

Number the four-carbon chain from the end nearest the branch. The same methyl group is at C-2 from one end and C-3 from the other. Choose the lower locant: 2-methylbutane. The two written names describe the same connectivity; they are not two isomers.

A proposed “2-ethylpropane” also hides a four-carbon continuous chain. Select that longer chain and the correct name is 2-methylbutane.

Quick check 3 · Count branches

In 2,2-dimethylpropane, how many carbon atoms are there altogether?

Show answer and explanation
The propane parent has 3 carbons, and the two methyl groups add 1 each: 5 carbon atoms. The molecular formula is C₅H₁₂.

Check for duplicates: Identify which carbons are bonded to which. Turning a sketch or tracing it from a different end does not change connectivity. When relevant, a reflected drawing may need stereochemical analysis; this lesson compares the carbon connectivity of simple acyclic alkanes.

6. Structure and physical properties

Alkanes are largely nonpolar and interact mainly through London dispersion forces. Within a comparable straight-chain series, boiling point generally rises as carbon number and molecular size increase. For isomers with the same molecular formula, greater branching often lowers boiling point because shape changes how effectively molecules interact.

Example · Compare isomers

Hexane boils at a higher temperature than 2,2-dimethylbutane. Both are C₆H₁₄, but the compact branched isomer generally has weaker effective intermolecular attractions. Boiling-point trends are not a universal prediction for every physical property; melting points can behave differently.

Alkanes are relatively unreactive compared with many unsaturated hydrocarbons, but they can undergo combustion and free-radical substitution under suitable conditions.

9. Key takeaways

  • Acyclic alkanes have formula CnH2n+2 and only C–C and C–H single bonds.
  • Constitutional isomers have the same molecular formula and different connectivity.
  • C₄H₁₀ has 2, C₅H₁₂ has 3, and C₆H₁₄ has 5 constitutional isomers.
  • To name a simple branched alkane, select the longest continuous chain, number to give the branch the lower locant, and name the substituent.
  • Rotation or reversal of a drawing does not produce a different connectivity.

Before the quiz, draw both C₄ isomers and all three C₅ isomers; check carbon and hydrogen counts and compare their connections.
