1. Alkanes and constitutional isomers

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons: their carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds. An acyclic alkane has formula CnH2n+2. A saturated hydrocarbon containing exactly one ring has formula CnH2n. Additional rings or multiple bonds require a different formula.

CompoundTypeFormula
pentaneopen chainC₅H₁₂
cyclopentaneone ringC₅H₁₀

Constitutional isomers share a molecular formula but have different atom-to-atom connections. Stereoisomers share connectivity but differ in three-dimensional arrangement.

Checkpoint 1 · Count isomers

How many constitutional isomers have the formula C₅H₁₂? Name them.

Show answer
Three: pentane, 2-methylbutane and 2,2-dimethylpropane. Each has five carbons and twelve hydrogens, with different carbon connections.

2. Alkyl groups

An alkyl group can be described as an alkane with one H removed. Its free bond marks the attachment point: –CH₃ is methyl and –CH₂CH₃ is ethyl.

Checkpoint 2 · One alkane, two attachment points

What two propyl groups can be derived from propane by removing H from an end carbon or from the middle carbon?

Show answer
An end gives propan-1-yl (–CH₂CH₂CH₃); the middle gives propan-2-yl (–CH(CH₃)₂, isopropyl). Both have formula C₃H₇– but differ at the attachment point.

3. Naming open-chain alkanes

  1. Trace the longest continuous parent chain. If equally long chains are possible in these simple cases, choose the chain with more substituents.
  2. Compare the sorted substituent locant sets from both ends; choose the lower number at the first difference.
  3. Name and number each substituent; use di-, tri-, or tetra- for repeats.
  4. List different substituents alphabetically, ignoring multiplying prefixes. Use commas between numbers and hyphens between numbers and words.

Checkpoint 3 · Numbering

Compare locant sets 2,3,5 and 2,4,5. Which is preferred?

Show answer
Choose 2,3,5: the first locants tie, but 3 is lower than 4 at the next position. Do not use the sum of locants.

4. Cycloalkanes and ring naming

For the simple examples in this chapter, use the ring as parent if it has at least as many carbon atoms as the attached acyclic chain. If the chain has more, the ring can be named as a cycloalkyl substituent. One substituent on a simple parent ring does not need locant 1.

Structural and skeletal view

Six ring carbons give cyclohexane; the one-carbon group is methyl. The name is methylcyclohexane. The polygon represents connectivity; cyclohexane itself is not planar.

For multiple substituents, compare all valid ring numbering directions at the first difference. If complete locant sets tie, assign the lower number to the substituent cited first alphabetically. Alphabetical order is used after the locant set is chosen.

Checkpoint 4 · Ring or chain?

A straight four-carbon chain is attached at its end to a three-carbon ring. Which is the parent and what is the name?

Show answer
The longer four-carbon chain is butane; the ring is cyclopropyl. The name is 1-cyclopropylbutane. Here 1 locates the ring on the chain.

Ring strain: cyclopropane has significant angle and torsional strain; cyclohexane can adopt a low-strain chair. Cyclobutane puckers, so a square skeletal drawing does not give its exact bond angles.

5. Cis–trans isomerism

For a specified pair of substituents on different ring carbons, cis means the same face; trans means opposite faces. The two substituents may be identical or different.

Same face · cis

Both methyl bonds are solid wedges.

Opposite faces · trans

One methyl bond is solid and one is dashed.

A solid wedge projects toward the viewer and a hashed wedge projects away. Ordinary lines without stereochemical information do not specify a cis/trans relationship. A chair flip changes axial to equatorial and vice versa, but preserves each group’s up/down orientation and the cis/trans relationship.

Checkpoint 5 · A ring flip

A substituent is equatorial down. What is it after a chair flip?

Show answer
Axial down. Its axial/equatorial position changes; its face, down, stays the same.

Challenge · Structure and name

Write a condensed formula for 2,2-dimethylbutane and count its carbons. Why is its parent butane?

Show answer and explanation
CH₃C(CH₃)₂CH₂CH₃. It has six carbon atoms in total. The longest continuous chain contains four carbon atoms; the two remaining carbons are separate methyl groups on C-2.
