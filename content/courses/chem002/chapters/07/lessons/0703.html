1. Benzene and aromaticity

Benzene, C₆H₆, is an aromatic six-carbon ring with six delocalized π electrons. Its two Kekulé drawings are resonance contributors: the actual molecule is a resonance hybrid with equivalent C–C bonds.

Worked review · Methylbenzene

Condensed: C₆H₅CH₃

The structural formula shows one Kekulé contributor; the circle in the skeletal formula represents delocalization. The ring has one methyl substituent, so the name is methylbenzene (toluene). No position number is needed for this single substituent.

Aromatic compounds are not limited to benzene rings or hydrocarbons. In the introductory monocyclic Hückel model, a cyclic, approximately planar, continuously conjugated system is aromatic when it has 4n + 2 π electrons. Benzene has six, with n = 1.

2. Phenyl, benzyl, and ring positions

Phenyl is C₆H₅–, attached through a ring carbon. Benzyl is C₆H₅CH₂–, attached through the CH₂ carbon. For example, C₆H₅Cl is chlorobenzene, whereas C₆H₅CH₂Cl is benzyl chloride.

Relative ring positionsCommon prefixMeaning
1,2ortho (o-)Adjacent ring carbons
1,3meta (m-)Separated by one ring carbon

3. Phenols and naming

A phenol has an –OH group attached directly to an aromatic ring carbon. C₆H₅OH is phenol; C₆H₅CH₂OH is benzyl alcohol, with a CH₂ between the ring and OH.

For the simple phenol-parent examples in this chapter, the OH-bearing carbon is C-1. Compare both numbering directions and choose the lower substituent locant set at the first difference. Then list different substituents alphabetically.

Worked review · Two substituents

Condensed: HOC₆H₃(NO₂)CH₃, with nitro at 2 and methyl at 4.

The two directions give substituent locants 2,4 and 4,6. Choose 2,4; write methyl before nitro alphabetically. The name is 4-methyl-2-nitrophenol.

Phenol is a weak acid, but is more acidic than ethanol in the usual comparison. After loss of H⁺, the phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance delocalization; ethoxide lacks the same aromatic resonance stabilization. Exact acidity depends on structure and solvent.

Some aromatic compounds have higher-priority functional groups and may use a different parent name or a hydroxy prefix. The simple phenol rules here apply when phenol is the chosen parent.

4. Benzenediols: two OH groups

A benzene ring with two hydroxyl groups directly attached is a benzenediol, also called a dihydroxybenzene or dihydric phenol. Its three positional constitutional isomers have the same molecular formula, C₆H₆O₂, but differ in where the OH groups attach.

Numerical nameCommon nameRelationship
benzene-1,2-diolcatechol (pyrocatechol)ortho; adjacent
benzene-1,3-diolresorcinolmeta; separated by one ring carbon
benzene-1,4-diolhydroquinonepara; opposite

Catechol · 1,2

The two OH groups are adjacent.

Resorcinol · 1,3

One ring carbon separates the OH-bearing carbons.

Hydroquinone · 1,4

The two OH groups are opposite each other. The structural diagrams show one Kekulé contributor; the circle shows delocalization in the skeletal formulas.
