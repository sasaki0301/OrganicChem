Chapter 5 · Lesson 3

Alkenes: Applying Cis–Trans and E–Z Designations

Build on Lesson 2 by reading complete structures, ranking substituents and naming the configuration with confidence.

Quick reviewWorked examplesMixed drillsKey takeaways

1. Decide what the drawing tells you

  1. Find the C=C. For a simple acyclic alkene to have an E/Z pair, each double-bond carbon must have two different substituents.
  2. Identify the parent alkene and number it as in Lesson 1.
  3. For E/Z, use CIP rules to rank the two groups independently on each C=C carbon. Higher-priority groups together → Z; opposite → E.
  4. Use cis/trans in suitable simple cases where a matching pair is unambiguous. Compare that pair, not necessarily the CIP winners.
A condensed formula can identify connectivity without specifying geometry. Do not assign E or Z unless a stereochemical drawing, name or other information tells you the arrangement. Stereoisomers have the same connectivity but differ in spatial arrangement.

CIP reminders: compare directly attached atoms by atomic number, for example Br > Cl > C > H. If tied, compare attached-atom lists at the first difference: –CH₂CH₃ has [C,H,H] and –CH₃ has [H,H,H], so ethyl ranks higher. E/Z is useful for any eligible double bond, including but-2-ene.

In the paired diagrams, structural views show grouped hydrogens. In skeletal views, unlabeled corners and ends are carbon; some H atoms are shown explicitly to help comparison. Parallel lines are the C=C.

2. Worked examples

Example 1 · Matching groups

Same side: cis and Z

In but-2-ene, each double-bond carbon has CH₃ and H. The two CH₃ groups are on the same side → cis-but-2-ene. Carbon outranks H on both ends, so the CIP winners are also together → (Z)-but-2-ene.

Example 2 · Matching groups

Opposite sides: trans and E

The CH₃ groups are opposite → trans-but-2-ene. Each CH₃ outranks H, so the higher-priority groups are opposite → (E)-but-2-ene.

Example 3 · Different priority atoms

Br/H versus CH₃/H

  1. Both double-bond carbons have two different groups.
  2. Br outranks H on the left; C of CH₃ outranks H on the right.
  3. Br and CH₃ are on the same side → Z. The parent chain has three carbons, with Br and C=C at C-1.

(Z)-1-bromoprop-1-ene. The two H groups also form a matching pair, so cis/trans can describe their relationship; it is not true that all four substituents here are different.

Example 4 · Parent name and priorities

Cl/CH₃ versus H/ethyl

The parent containing C=C has five carbons and the double bond begins at C-2. Chlorine is at C-2. Cl outranks C on the left; C of ethyl outranks H on the right. The winners are opposite → (E)-2-chloropent-2-ene.

Example 5 · Same first atom

Methyl versus ethyl

The parent is 3-methylpent-2-ene. On the left, CH₃ outranks H. On the right both groups attach through C, so compare [C,H,H] for ethyl with [H,H,H] for methyl: ethyl wins. The winners are opposite → (E)-3-methylpent-2-ene.

There is a methyl group on each end; the four groups are not all different. E/Z remains applicable because within each end the two groups differ.

Example 6 · Same connectivity, no E/Z

Propene

One C=C carbon has two H atoms. No E/Z pair exists; the name is propene (prop-1-ene).

Example 7 · Compare the reference groups

Cis does not always mean Z

The two methyl groups are on the same side, so their relative arrangement is cis. For E/Z, however, Cl outranks methyl on the left and methyl outranks H on the right. The higher-priority groups are opposite → E. The two labels compare different groups.

Practice 6 · Stability in a specific comparison

Which is generally more stable: (E)-hex-3-ene or (Z)-hex-3-ene? Explain without treating E as a universal stability rule.

Show answer and explanation
For this particular pair, (E)-hex-3-ene generally has the ethyl groups on opposite sides and is more stable, consistent with less steric crowding. E and Z describe priority, not size by definition; other interactions and structures affect stability.

Challenge · Cis does not always mean Z

In Example 7, why is the matching-methyl relationship cis while the CIP designation is E?

Show answer
The methyl groups are on the same side, so their relationship is cis. CIP selects Cl on the left and methyl on the right; these higher-priority groups lie on opposite sides, giving E.

4. Key takeaways

  • Check that each double-bond carbon has two different substituents before assigning E/Z.
  • Rank groups separately at each end; E/Z can apply even when a group appears at both ends.
  • Z compares higher-priority groups on the same side; E means opposite sides.
  • Cis/trans compares an appropriate matching pair; cis need not be Z when that pair is not the CIP winners.
  • A formula alone may leave configuration unspecified.
