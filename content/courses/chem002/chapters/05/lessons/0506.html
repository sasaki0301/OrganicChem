Cycloalkynes

Explore how a ring changes the geometry of an alkyne, then practice reading and naming simple cycloalkynes with structural and skeletal formulas.

1. What is a cycloalkyne?

A cycloalkyne is a cyclic hydrocarbon with a carbon–carbon triple bond within a ring. The triple bond has one σ and two π bonds. Cyclooctyne contains eight ring carbons and one C≡C.

Condensed ring notation: cyclo-[C≡C–(CH₂)₆]. The final CH₂ connects back to the first C, closing the ring. The bracketed notation is used here to make ring closure clear.

In a skeletal formula every unlabeled corner represents carbon; its attached hydrogens are omitted. In the structural formula the carbon groups are shown. The three parallel lines indicate C≡C.

Formula shortcut: A hydrocarbon with exactly one ring, one triple bond within that ring, and no other multiple bonds has formula CnH2n−4. For cyclooctyne, n = 8, giving C₈H₁₂. Additional rings or multiple bonds change the formula.

2. Why is the ring strained?

In an ordinary acyclic alkyne, each triple-bond carbon is described as sp-hybridized, and the bonds around it are approximately linear (180°). A ring connects the two ends and forces the carbon framework to bend away from the preferred arrangement, contributing to ring strain.

The ring diagrams on this page show connectivity; the polygon is not a measured three-dimensional shape or bond-angle diagram. Actual rings can pucker, and angles depend on the molecule.

Worked comparison · Cyclohexyne and cyclooctyne

The six-membered unsubstituted ring imposes a greater geometric constraint than an eight-membered ring in this comparison. Cyclohexyne is highly reactive and is typically generated and trapped as a transient intermediate; cyclooctyne is the smallest unsubstituted cycloalkyne commonly isolated as a free compound. Cyclooctyne still has significant strain.

Do not treat eight carbons as an absolute stability boundary: substituents, heteroatoms, ring fusion and conditions can change stability. A larger ring does not guarantee an exact strain value or reaction rate.

An application: copper-free click chemistry

Some cyclooctyne-based reagents react selectively with organic azides in a strain-promoted azide–alkyne cycloaddition, producing a triazole without a copper catalyst. This can be useful for labeling biomolecules. The particular reagents and conditions matter; not every strained alkyne reacts equally fast or selectively.

3. Naming simple cycloalkynes

For the monocyclic hydrocarbons shown here, with one endocyclic triple bond and alkyl substituents:

  1. Choose the ring parent. Count its carbons and use cyclo- and -yne.
  2. Number the triple bond as C-1 and C-2. Try both choices of which triple-bond carbon is C-1.
  3. Compare substituent locant sets. Sort each set and select the lower number at the first difference. If complete sets tie, the alphabetically first substituent gets the lower locant.
  4. Assemble the name. List different substituents alphabetically; use di-, tri-, and so on for repeats.

For a ring with one triple bond, cyclooctyne and cyclooct-1-yne identify the same parent; we show the locant explicitly in the examples. More complex structures require additional naming rules.

A ring carbon in C≡C already has a triple bond to one ring carbon and a single bond to the other. It cannot carry an additional alkyl branch while maintaining ordinary carbon valence. Place branches on suitable ring carbons.

Example 1 · Identify the ring

Condensed: cyclo-[C≡C–(CH₂)₆]

  1. Count eight ring carbons: cyclooct-.
  2. The single triple bond is between C-1 and C-2.
  3. The name is cyclooct-1-yne, commonly written cyclooctyne.

Example 2 · Number toward the substituent

Condensed: cyclo-[C≡C–CH(CH₃)–(CH₂)₅]

  1. The ring has eight carbons, including both carbons of C≡C.
  2. With the numbering shown, methyl is at C-3. The opposite numbering places it at C-8.
  3. Choose the lower locant: 3-methylcyclooct-1-yne.

Example 3 · Compare two locant sets

Condensed: cyclo-[C≡C–CH(CH₂CH₃)–CH₂–CH₂–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₂]

  1. Number the triple bond 1 and 2. The direction shown gives ethyl at 3 and methyl at 6: set 3,6.
  2. Reverse numbering gives methyl at 5 and ethyl at 8: set 5,8. Compare the first numbers: 3 is lower than 5.
  3. List ethyl before methyl: 3-ethyl-6-methylcyclooct-1-yne.

5. Key takeaways

  • A cycloalkyne contains a C≡C within a ring.
  • Ring geometry can bend the alkyne framework away from its preferred linear arrangement and cause strain.
  • Unsubstituted cyclooctyne is commonly isolable but still strained; stability depends on the particular structure and conditions.
  • For a single-ring, single-triple-bond hydrocarbon, CnH2n−4 applies.
  • In the simple naming examples, C≡C occupies C-1 and C-2, and substituent locant sets determine the direction.
