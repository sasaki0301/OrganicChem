Chapter 5 · Lesson 7

Mixed Alkene–Alkyne Compounds

Combine what you know about alkenes and alkynes. Identify both multiple bonds, compare numbering directions, and name the compound using structural, condensed and skeletal formulas.

1. Recognizing an enyne

An enyne is a hydrocarbon containing both a carbon–carbon double bond (C=C) and a carbon–carbon triple bond (C≡C). In a skeletal formula, an unlabeled line end or corner is carbon, and hydrogens on carbon are omitted.

Condensed formula: CH₂=CH–CH₂–C≡CH

The drawings show the same five-carbon molecule. Two lines indicate the double bond; three lines indicate the triple bond. The carbon atoms on either side of C≡C and their immediately attached atoms form an approximately linear region.

This lesson focuses on acyclic hydrocarbons with exactly one C=C and one C≡C in a single continuous parent chain. Compounds with several multiple bonds, rings, or higher-priority functional groups require additional parent-selection and numbering rules. In some structures, no one chain can contain both multiple bonds.

2. Naming the examples in this lesson

  1. Choose the parent chain. Find the longest continuous chain that contains both C=C and C≡C. Follow the path around corners; do not assume the straightest-looking path is the longest.
  2. Compare both numbering directions. Record the lower-numbered carbon of each multiple bond. Sort the two locants for each direction and choose the lower set at the first difference.
  3. Break a complete tie. If the sorted multiple-bond sets are identical, number so the C=C has the lower locant.
  4. Build the parent name. Give the root, then the double-bond locant with -en-, followed by the triple-bond locant with -yne: for example, pent-1-en-4-yne. En is written before yne even when the triple-bond locant is smaller.
  5. Add substituents. Identify branches and their locants; use di-, tri-, etc. for repeats and alphabetize different substituent names.
Two different ideas: Compare both multiple-bond locants when numbering. The double bond receives the lower number only when the overall multiple-bond locant sets tie; the word part en is written first in either case.

3. Worked examples

Example 1 · A numbering tie

Condensed: CH₂=CH–CH₂–C≡CH

  1. Count five connected carbons: pent-.
  2. From the alkene end: C=C at 1 and C≡C at 4, sorted set 1,4. From the alkyne end: C≡C at 1 and C=C at 4, also 1,4.
  3. The sets tie, so give C=C the lower locant. Name: pent-1-en-4-yne.

Example 2 · The triple bond gets the lower locant

Condensed: HC≡C–CH=C(CH₃)₂

  1. The longest chain containing both multiple bonds has five carbons. The second methyl group is a substituent.
  2. Number from the triple-bond end: C≡C at 1, C=C at 3 → set 1,3. From the other end: C=C at 2, C≡C at 4 → set 2,4.
  3. Since 1 is lower than 2, choose 1,3. The methyl group is on C-4. Write en before yne: 4-methylpent-3-en-1-yne.

Example 3 · The parent chain turns at a junction

Condensed: CH₂=CH–C≡C–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃

  1. Follow the path containing both multiple bonds. At the junction, the upper route adds two carbons; the lower route adds one. The upper route is part of the seven-carbon parent.
  2. From the C=C end, multiple-bond locants are 1 and 3; from the other end they are 4 and 6. Choose 1,3.
  3. The one-carbon route is methyl at C-5. Name: 5-methylhept-1-en-3-yne.

Example 4 · A second turning chain

Condensed: CH₂=CH–C≡C–CH(CH₃)–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃

  1. Following the path containing both multiple bonds through both junctions gives an eight-carbon parent; each remaining short branch is a methyl group.
  2. Number from the C=C end: double at 1 and triple at 3; the opposite direction gives 7 and 5, respectively.
  3. The methyl groups are at C-5 and C-6. Name: 5,6-dimethyloct-1-en-3-yne.

Practice 6 · Explain your choice

Two numbering directions give C=C at 3 and C≡C at 1, or C=C at 2 and C≡C at 4. Which direction wins?

Show answer
Choose C=C at 3 and C≡C at 1. Its sorted multiple-bond set 1,3 is lower than 2,4 at the first difference. The double-bond tie-break does not apply because the sets are different.

Challenge · Apply the tie-break

Both directions give the sorted set 1,4. One puts C=C at 1; the other puts C=C at 4. Which is preferred?

Show answer
Put C=C at 1. With identical sorted multiple-bond locants, the double bond gets the lower locant.

5. Key takeaways

  • An enyne has both C=C and C≡C.
  • For the examples here, select the longest continuous chain containing both multiple bonds.
  • Number to give the lower sorted set of multiple-bond locants; when the sets tie, give C=C the lower number.
  • Write -en- before -yne even if the triple-bond locant is lower.
  • Count through corners and identify substituents after choosing the parent.
