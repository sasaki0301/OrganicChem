1. Atomic structure and electron configuration

  • Atomic number Z is the number of protons; it identifies the element.
  • Mass number A is protons + neutrons; for one isotope, n = A − Z. It differs from a periodic-table average atomic mass.
  • Isotopes share a proton count and differ in neutron count. Ions differ in electron count from the neutral atom.
  • Charge = p − e. Only a neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons.
  • Orbitals are quantum descriptions associated with electron probability distributions, not fixed circular tracks.

For the main-group examples in this chapter, valence electrons are those in the highest occupied principal shell. Neutral carbon is 1s² 2s² 2p²: 2 + 2 = 4 valence electrons, not merely the final 2p².

Checkpoint · Sodium atom and ion

Sodium-23 has Z = 11. Give p, n, and e for neutral Na and Na⁺, then write the ground-state configuration of each.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Both have 11 p and 12 n. Neutral Na has 11 e and configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹. Na⁺ has 10 e and configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. Check Na⁺: 11 − 10 = +1.

2. Bonding, Lewis structures, and polarity

ModelAttraction or shared electronsExample
IonicElectrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ionsNaCl crystal: Na⁺ and Cl⁻ in a lattice
CovalentShared electron density attracted to both bonded nucleiH–H within H₂
MetallicMetal cores and delocalized electronsSolid copper

Electron transfer can account for simple ion formation, but ionic bonding is the attraction between ions. A coordinate bond is a covalent bond whose shared pair is supplied by one partner during formation.

Lewis structures account for valence electrons, bonds, lone pairs, and charges. VSEPR then uses bonding regions and lone pairs to predict a local arrangement. In H₂O, oxygen has four electron regions: tetrahedral electron-region geometry and a bent molecular shape, about 104.5°.

Bond polarity is not the same as molecular polarity. CO₂ has polar C=O bonds, but its linear arrangement makes their dipoles cancel; bent H₂O has a net dipole.

Breaking a bond requires energy; forming a bond releases energy. The overall energy change depends on both. The octet rule is a useful guide, not a universal rule.

Checkpoint · Reason from shape

Why is CO₂ nonpolar even though its C=O bonds are polar?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

The two identical bond dipoles point in opposite directions in linear CO₂ and cancel. H₂O is bent, so its two O–H bond dipoles do not cancel.

Checkpoint · Ethene

Assign the local model at each carbon in CH₂=CH₂. Count all σ and π bonds in the whole molecule.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Each carbon has three neighbors → sp². Four C–H bonds contribute 4 σ; the C=C contributes 1 σ + 1 π. Total: 5 σ and 1 π. The double connection is only one electron region around each carbon.

4. Read and draw chemical structures

RepresentationWhat it showsImportant limitation
Molecular formulaNumber of each kind of atomDoes not uniquely show connectivity
Lewis structureBonds, lone pairs, and chargeDoes not by itself fully specify 3D geometry
CondensedConnected groups, with branches often in parenthesesNot every bond is drawn
SkeletalCarbon framework and explicitly written heteroatomsMost C labels and C–H bonds are omitted

In a skeletal formula, each unlabeled endpoint, corner, and junction represents C. Add enough H to give an ordinary neutral carbon four bonds, counting double bonds twice. A labeled atom such as O is not an extra carbon; H on O or N is usually shown.

Ethanol: the green C labels are teaching aids. Condensed CH₃CH₂OH; molecular C₂H₆O. Include the H on oxygen.

Different structures can have the same molecular formula: ethanol CH₃CH₂OH and dimethyl ether CH₃OCH₃ are constitutional isomers. Wedges and hashed wedges can add spatial information.

Checkpoint · Count a branch

For CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃, count carbon and hydrogen atoms. What does the parenthesized CH₃ indicate?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

There are 4 carbons and 10 hydrogens, so C₄H₁₀. The parenthesized CH₃ is a branch attached to the immediately preceding CH.

5. Organic nomenclature: the introductory workflow

  1. Read and select: identify the principal characteristic group and relevant multiple bonds; choose an eligible continuous parent chain.
  2. Number: in the simple examples studied, give the principal suffix group its lowest locant, then the multiple bond, then compare substituent locants at the first point of difference.
  3. Name the attached groups: use a locant for each occurrence and di-, tri-, or tetra- for repeated identical groups.
  4. Assemble: alphabetize different simple substituent names, ignoring di-, tri-, and tetra- for alphabetization; add the parent and ending.
  5. Check: redraw the name and compare it with the structure.
This sequence is an introductory guide for the simple open-chain examples in Lesson 5. Competing parent chains, multiple characteristic groups, rings, and stereochemistry need the fuller IUPAC rules. A longer chain that omits a principal suffix group is not automatically the parent.

For CH₃CH(CH₃)CH(Cl)CH₂CH₃, the parent is pentane. Numbering gives methyl at 2 and chloro at 3; cite chloro before methyl: 3-chloro-2-methylpentane. The name leaves any stereochemistry unspecified.

Among the selected groups reviewed in Lesson 5, an acid group has priority over an OH group for the principal suffix: HOOC–CH(OH)–CH₃ is 2-hydroxypropanoic acid. The abbreviated group-priority list is not a complete hierarchy.

Checkpoint · Compare numbering

For the same alkane parent, compare substituent locants 2,2,4 and 2,4,4. Which set wins?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

2,2,4. The first entries tie; at the first difference the second entries are 2 and 4, so 2 is preferred. Compare ordered locants, not their sums.

6. One structure connects the chapter

Integrated example

Propene: CH₂=CH–CH₃

  1. Structure and atoms: the molecule has three carbon atoms and 2 + 1 + 3 = six H atoms, so C₃H₆.
  2. Regions and hybridization: C-1 and C-2 each have three neighbors → sp². C-3 has four neighbors → sp³. Count the C=C as one region around each of its two carbons.
  3. Whole-molecule bond count: six C–H bonds give 6 σ; two C–C connections give 2 σ; the C=C adds 1 π. Total = 8 σ and 1 π.
  4. Naming: the parent has three carbons and one double bond: propene. Numbering from the nearer end places the double bond at C-1; its position is unambiguous in propene.
  5. Model and shape: the two double-bonded carbons are approximately trigonal planar. Twisting the double bond substantially disrupts the π overlap in the localized model.

A carbon’s electron-region count, a bond’s σ/π composition, and the molecule’s total atoms are related, but they are different counting tasks.

7. Mixed practice: retrieve, explain, apply

Try each item before opening the explanation. Each answer identifies a lesson to revisit if the reasoning is unclear.

1 · Count particles

Magnesium-24 has Z = 12. Find p, n, and e in neutral magnesium-24 and in Mg²⁺ of the same isotope.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Both have 12 p and 12 n. The neutral atom has 12 e; Mg²⁺ has 10 e. Charges: 12 − 12 = 0 and 12 − 10 = +2. Revisit Lesson 1.

2 · Configure and count

Write the ground-state configuration for neutral O, Z = 8. How many valence electrons does it have?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

1s² 2s² 2p⁴. The highest occupied shell is n = 2; 2 + 4 = 6 valence electrons. Revisit Lesson 1.

3 · Explain a conducting solid

Why does copper conduct as a solid while solid NaCl conducts poorly?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Copper has mobile delocalized electrons. NaCl has charged ions, but they are fixed in the solid lattice; its ions can carry charge when molten or dissolved. Revisit Lesson 2.

4 · Electron geometry versus shape

NH₃ has three N–H bonds and one lone pair on N. Give its electron-region geometry, molecular shape, and simple hybridization model.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Four regions give a tetrahedral electron-region arrangement and an sp³ approximation. The atom positions make a trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape. Revisit Lessons 2–3.

5 · Count a charged Lewis structure

How many valence electrons and N lone pairs are in the standard Lewis structure of NH₄⁺?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

5 + 4(1) − 1 = 8 valence electrons. Four N–H bonds use all eight, leaving zero lone pairs on N. Bracket the ion with overall +. Revisit Lessons 2 and 4.

6 · Count a triple bond and its hydrogens

For HC≡CH, assign each carbon’s local model and count all σ and π bonds in the molecule.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Each carbon has two neighbors → sp. Two C–H σ bonds plus one C–C σ bond give 3 σ; the triple connection adds 2 π. Revisit Lesson 3.

7 · Same formula, different structure

Ethanol CH₃CH₂OH and dimethyl ether CH₃OCH₃ share C₂H₆O. Are they the same compound?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

No. Their atom connections differ, so they are constitutional isomers. A molecular formula alone does not specify connectivity. Revisit Lesson 4.

8 · Read a skeletal formula

An unlabeled four-position zigzag has three single-bond segments and no branches. Write its condensed and molecular formulas.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Four carbon positions give CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃, or C₄H₁₀. Each end is CH₃ and each interior carbon is CH₂. Revisit Lesson 4.

9 · Name a branched chain

Name CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ using the simple workflow.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

The longest continuous chain contains four carbons; the remaining methyl is on C-2: 2-methylbutane. Revisit Lesson 5.

10 · Alphabetical order

Name CH₃CH(CH₃)CH(Cl)CH₂CH₃. Why does the name not begin with 2-methyl?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

The parent is pentane, with methyl at 2 and chloro at 3: 3-chloro-2-methylpentane. Chloro is cited first alphabetically, while its own locant stays 3. Revisit Lesson 5.

11 · Principal group and numbering

For HOOC–CH(OH)–CH₃, identify the acid suffix and name the compound.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

The carboxyl carbon is C-1 and the OH is at C-2. Acid is the principal group in this pair; OH is hydroxy-: 2-hydroxypropanoic acid. This does not specify stereochemistry. Revisit Lesson 5.

12 · Construct from a name

Write a condensed structure for 2,2-dimethylpropane. How many total carbons are present?

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

CH₃C(CH₃)₂CH₃. The parent has 3 carbons and two methyl branches add 2, giving 5 total carbons. The central carbon has four C–C bonds and no hydrogen. Revisit Lessons 4–5.

Final retrieval prompt

Explain how you would go from the condensed structure CH₂=CHCH₃ to its carbon hybridizations, whole-molecule σ / π count, and name.

Show answer and explanationHide answer and explanation

Each double-bonded carbon has three regions and is sp²; the CH₃ carbon has four and is sp³. The molecule has six C–H σ bonds and two C–C σ bonds, plus one π bond: 8 σ and 1 π. The three-carbon alkene is propene.
